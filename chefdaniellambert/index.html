02

Conversion (the assumption doing the work)

The gap between the conservative and optimistic case is five percentage points — 215 buyers versus 323, €32,250 versus €48,450. Nothing about your content changes that; the launch sequence does. That is why section six exists and why the DM follow-up in Phase 5 is the part that decides which end of the range you land on.

03

Price (the one to leave alone first time)

At €150 you need 215 buyers for the conservative case. Halve the price and you need 430 to stand still — that is 20% of your engaged audience, which is a far bigger ask than the price cut saves you. Hold €150 for launch one and let the finished product justify it.

One rule for reading page 5: the three products in the ladder are three routes to the same €32,250, not three revenue streams to add together. They would all be sold to the same 2,152 people. Pick one, launch it properly, then build the next.

Section four

Offer detail: the flagship

Option one · €150 · The big bet

The Comfort Chef Blueprint

What it is: a complete video system teaching everyday cooks how to build deep restaurant flavour, nail sauces and balance textures using supermarket staples.

Who it is for: food lovers and busy professionals who want to cook incredible meals for friends and family without spending three hours in the kitchen or washing ten pots.

Why it sells: it answers the exact pain point behind your biggest view counts. People crave rich, impressive food; what they lack is a bulletproof system to pull it off.

What is inside

  • Flavour layering — the order of operations that makes a sausage rigatoni taste like it was finished in a restaurant pass, not a home hob.
  • Sauces without fear — the handful of sauces that carry most comfort dishes, each one built in the same pan as the main event.
  • Texture — the crisp-versus-soft contrast that made the Guinness hash browns land: soak, dry, starch, fry.
  • One-pan discipline — how to design a dinner so nothing else ends up in the sink.
  • The shop — which supermarket staples to always have in, so a good dinner is never a special trip.

The promise

Pick any night of the week, open the press, and put something on the table that people ask you about — with one pan and no chef school.

First step to build it: do not film a curriculum. Film one module — flavour layering, using the sausage rigatoni you have already proven — as five short lessons on your phone in your own kitchen. Sell that module as the Blueprint's opening chapter during launch week and film the rest while people are already inside. The audience that comments "Recipe" is telling you they will happily learn from you in short, self-contained pieces.

Section five

Launch plan: phases 1–3

The structure from the audit, with what to post at each stage. Phases one to three happen before a price is ever mentioned.

01

Build up — interaction is the only goal

Stories all week, aimed at the pain point rather than the product. A poll: "Do you buy the jarred sauce because homemade feels like too much hassle?" A question box: "What one dish do you want to nail this year?" Re-share the pistachio paste reel with a sticker asking whether they would want every sold-out item broken down like that. Read the answers back on camera — the words people use here become the sales copy later.

02

Invitation — move them somewhere you own

Announce that something is coming and invite them into a private group. Use the mechanic they already know: instead of "comment 'Recipe'", it is "comment 'LUSH' and I'll send you the link". Same habit, new destination. Tease that there is a time-limited bonus inside for the people in the group first, and say plainly that the detail only gets revealed in there.

03

Anticipation — teach once, for free, inside the group

Give the group one real lesson: the flavour-layering order on the sausage rigatoni, filmed properly, start to plate. Then make the timeline unmissable — the day doors open, the day they close, and what happens in between. Nobody in that group should be surprised on launch day.

Why this order matters: phases one to three exist to turn 2,152 scattered engagers into a group you can message directly. The conversion assumption on page 3 only holds if you can reach those people without the algorithm's permission.

Section five, continued

Launch plan: phases 4–6

The selling window. Short, dated, and closed for real at the end of it.

04

Reveal — leave no ambiguity

The full breakdown, in the group first: what is included, the price (€150), the exclusive bonus — the Viral Kitchen Vault pistachio paste masterclass — and the exact open and close dates. Say what it is not, as well as what it is. People do not buy when they are unsure what they are getting.

05

Conversion — the DMs are the shop floor

Send the enrolment link. Run a poll with two options: "I'm in" and "I have a question". Then answer every single question personally, one to one. This is the phase that decides whether you land at 215 buyers or 323 — the difference between the two scenarios on page 3 is made here, not in the content.

06

Urgency — and a close that is real

Countdown messages at 24, 12, 6 and 2 hours, then a clear "closed". Each one names what they are choosing to keep: another year of the jarred sauce and the ten pots. Then honour the close. Reopening after a deadline costs you the next launch, and there will be a next launch.

Keep it tight

A three to five day window with a hard close. A long, open-ended launch trains people to wait, and waiting is how a 10% conversion becomes a 3% one.

Keep filming

Do not stop posting recipes during launch week. The reels are what bring new people into the group mid-launch, and they are the proof that the method works.
