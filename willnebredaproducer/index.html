Why in this order

The guide and the workshop are not the money — rung three is. Their job is to turn followers into buyers while you carry on mixing, so that when you open the incubator in spring you are selling a $1,500 programme to people who have already paid you $29 or $197 and liked what arrived. That is a far shorter conversation than selling a cohort cold.

It also protects the number on the cover. Every guide buyer tells you, in their reply and their release date, whether the cohort should run in April or July, and whether $1,500 is the right figure for this audience before you commit eight weeks of evenings to it.

Offer detail · rung three

Release-Ready Production Incubator

$1,500 · eight weeks · 13 – 20 spots · spring or summer 2027

What is inside

  • Eight weeks, small group, applications only
  • Weekly live mix reviews — their track, on your speakers, in front of the group
  • Track diagnostics: what is wrong, why, and the order to fix it in
  • Direct feedback on their actual sessions between calls
  • Release strategy guidance, so the finished record lands on launch day

Who it is for

Independent rock, folk and singer-songwriter artists who have recorded tracks at home and are stuck in mix paralysis — months of tweaking, objectivity gone, terrified of releasing something flat that cannot compete on streaming.

The promise: you arrive with an unreleased track and leave in eight weeks with a release-ready mix and a plan for putting it out — with me listening to your work every single week, so you are never guessing on your own.

Why it sells

It solves the exact frustration your top content exposes. Artists spend months obsessing over mixes, lose objectivity, and still release music that cannot compete. Direct access and live feedback replace that with certainty — and certainty is what $1,500 buys.

The first step to build it

The eight weeks already exist across your feed. Write the week-by-week from what you have posted: the seven rules as weeks one and two, top-down workflow and the ego problem in three and four, vocals in five and six, the release checklist in seven and eight. Then fix the dates now, name them at the end of the guide and the workshop, and open applications in spring.

Estimate: 13 spots × $1,500 = $19,500 conservative; 20 × $1,500 = $30,000 at the ceiling. Viable at five.

The six phases for the cohort launch

  • 1. Build up. Stories, polls and question boxes on the pain points — forty hours on a vocal, mixing in solo, the release that went nowhere. Interaction is the whole goal.
  • 2. Invitation. Announce something is coming and invite people into a private WhatsApp or Telegram group where the details land first. Tease a time-limited bonus.
  • 3. Anticipation. Inside the group: what is coming, what artists will walk out with, and a timeline that is crystal clear so nobody misses the opening.
  • 4. Reveal. The full breakdown — what is included, the bonus, the price, and the open and close dates. No ambiguity left anywhere.
  • 5. Conversion. Send the application link for the limited spots, run a poll for who is in and who has questions, then follow up one-to-one in the DMs. Most of the thirteen close here.
  • 6. Urgency. Countdowns at 24h, 12h, 6h, 2h and closed, each one naming what staying out costs them. The close must be real — reopening it destroys the next launch.

Phases as set out in the audit, sequenced around your spring or summer timing.

Section six

The first post

Before you build anything, test the demand. Three slides, black background, white text — the format that already works for you. Post it this week.

Slide one · the hook

You have spent forty hours tweaking that one vocal track. And deep down, you know it still does not sound like a real record.

Slide two · the agitation

It is not your gear and it is not a missing plugin. You are tweaking in solo, second-guessing every EQ move, and quietly killing the emotion of the song. More hours inside your DAW will not fix a broken mix workflow.

Slide three · the call

Next year I am putting together a small, private group of fifteen artists — we take your unreleased tracks and turn them into release-ready, streaming-grade records, with my direct guidance every week. If that is you, reply I WANT and I will tell you first.

Caption

Being an artist myself, I know what it costs to hand your music over. This is the opposite of that — you stay in the room the whole way. Reply I WANT if you want the details before anyone else.

What to do with the replies

Replies are not sales. Treat the count as a read on interest and DM every single person: what are you working on, when do you want it out, what is the bit you are stuck on. Those conversations tell you the price, the dates and the wording of the offer months before you commit to it — and they are the first names on the list you sell the guide to in November.
