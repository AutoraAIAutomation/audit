The opportunity

The gap in what you offer

Your paid help only reaches bikes that physically get to your workshop. Your method reaches everyone who watches. Nothing sits in between.

Observed
  • About 117,000 followers watching detailed repair videos: grips, wheel rebuilds, tool reviews.
  • A paid workshop service already exists, alongside your tool partnerships (@6miltools, @gt85spray) and the independent shop, @shifta.bike.
  • There is no teaching product. The method is free in the videos; paid help needs the bike on your bench.
Inferred, not yet confirmed
  • Some viewers can't use the workshop because of distance or your capacity.
  • You haven't said how many. This audit treats it as the question to test, not a fact.
In your words (DM, 24/09)

You pick each job by "what the bike actually needs, what will make the biggest difference to the rider, and what will make an interesting transformation to film", and you try "to keep it genuine rather than forcing a repair purely for content".

You already take on "paid BMX repairs, restorations and custom builds through my workshop".

The transformation you could sell

Riders who can't get their bike to you become riders who can diagnose, repair and upgrade their own bikes with workshop-grade technique and the right tools.

Verdict: the free videos already show the method. The gap is a structured, paid version for riders who want to do the job themselves, step by step, on their own bike. It sits next to the workshop rather than competing with it.

Product 01 · £39

The Workshop-at-Home Series

A paid video series that follows one full restoration from stripped frame to finished bike. It is the same work you already film, sequenced and finished properly, with each step broken out:

1
Diagnosis. What the bike actually needs, the first question you already ask on every job.
2
What to replace and why. The decisions that make the biggest difference to the rider.
3
Tools. The exact kit for each step, with the tool list below.
4
The order of work. Strip, repair, rebuild, in the sequence a workshop uses.
The promise: do the whole job yourself, step by step, on your own bike.

Why it fits you: it keeps your own rule of choosing work that genuinely matters to the bike, rather than forcing a repair for content. First step: pick one restoration you are already planning to film, and film it with the series in mind.

The sequence

Launch plan

Four steps, in this order. Nothing gets built until your riders have told you they want it.

01

Ask first

Run a story poll or question box asking riders whether they would rather send their bike in or learn to do the job themselves. Then post the test carousel on the next page. This tests demand before anything is built.

02

Film one restoration for the series

Pick one restoration project you are already planning to film, and film it with the series in mind: diagnosis, parts, tools and order of work captured as separate steps.

03

Waitlist, then a real launch

Invite interested riders, starting with everyone who commented "BUILD", to a waitlist. Open the series to them first, with a clear launch date and a real close.

04

Decide on a second series

Use the replies and waitlist numbers to decide whether a second series is worth making.

What to watch

The estimate needs about 290 buyers. Your poll replies, "BUILD" comments and waitlist sign-ups are the first real evidence for or against that 0.25%, so count them before you film.
